CoronaryCCO.jl is a Julia port of **HCT-CCO**. It is a derivative work. The
algorithm, its constants, and its control flow come from the upstream authors
listed below. This port changes the language and the execution strategy. It does
not claim the method.

### Methods the algorithm rests on

- The original CCO method: W. Schreiner and P. F. Buxbaum, "Computer-optimization of vascular trees," *IEEE Transactions
  on Biomedical Engineering*, vol. 40, no. 5, pp. 482–491, 1993.
  <https://doi.org/10.1109/10.243413>
- The 3D extension: R. Karch, F. Neumann, M. Neumann and W. Schreiner, "A
  three-dimensional model for arterial tree representation, generated by
  constrained constructive optimization," *Computers in Biology and Medicine*,
  vol. 29, no. 1, pp. 19–38, 1999.
  <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-4825(98)00045-6>
- The bifurcation position/radius optimization: A. Kamiya and T. Togawa,
  "Optimal branching structure of the vascular tree," *Bulletin of
  Mathematical Biophysics*, vol. 34, no. 4, pp. 431–438, 1972.
  <https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02476705>

## Upstream dependencies that this port does NOT redistribute

HCT-CCO builds on the libraries below. CoronaryCCO.jl replaces each with Julia
code and contains none of their source. They are listed so that credit and
license terms stay visible. Where this port reproduces one of their numerical
procedures (for example the Ceres trust-region solve), it does so to match
upstream outputs, and the corresponding source file says so.

| Library | Role in HCT-CCO | License |
|---|---|---|
| [DGtal](https://github.com/DGtal-team/DGtal) | points, images, `.vol` I/O, distance transform | LGPL-3.0 |
| [Ceres Solver](https://github.com/ceres-solver/ceres-solver) | Kamiya bifurcation solve | BSD-3-Clause |
| [CLI11](https://github.com/CLIUtils/CLI11) | command-line parsing | BSD-3-Clause |
| numpy, vtk, matplotlib | Python post-processing | BSD-3-Clause / BSD-3-Clause / PSF-based |
